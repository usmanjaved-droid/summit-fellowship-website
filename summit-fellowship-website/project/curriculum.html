Module 01

Design for impact
at scale.

The foundational building blocks. Every cohort's models are reconstructed from these elements first.

Day 01 · Monday · 09:00 – 17:30

Most social enterprises know what they do. Far fewer can answer, in one sentence, what specific behavior change drives the outcome they care about — and which model elements actually produce that behavior.

This module is the most precise of the week. By lunchtime, every fellow has rewritten their mission to eight words and their Big Idea to six. By the end of day, the Behavior Map and the model's core elements are on paper.

The Theory of Change · simplified
Big Idea

  • 1.1

    Mission · 8 words, outcome-focused

    Strip every word until what's left names a specific outcome for a specific population. No nouns about activities. No "empowering" or "transforming". The mission either causes the outcome or it doesn't.

  • 1.2

    Big Idea · ≤ 6 words

    The compact, replicable mechanism. The thing that, if copy-pasted onto someone else's organization, would still produce the outcome.

  • 1.3

    Theory · idea → behavior → outcome

    The causal chain. What behavior change does your idea produce, in whom, that produces the outcome? If any link is fuzzy, the model isn't ready.

  • 1.4

    The Model · 4–6 core elements

    The minimum set of activities the model must do to produce the behavior change. Strip anything else.

  • 1.5

    Behavior map + drivers

    What gets in the way of the behavior change? What removes the barriers? Map drivers explicitly — most failed scale-ups failed at exactly one of them.

By the end of Day 1, every fellow has a one-pager: Mission, Big Idea, Theory, Model, Behaviors, Doer, and Payer.

Module 02

Scale strategy
+ the Scale Screen.

The four-question filter every Mulago-backed model has to earn a yes on.

Day 02 · Tuesday · 09:00 – 13:00

The Scale Screen is the framework at the heart of Mulago's investment thesis. It is not a maturity model — it is a yes/no filter. By the end of this module, fellows can defensibly answer all four questions about their own model.

The Four Enoughs · Mulago Scale Screen

Big enough
Simple enough
Cheap enough
Evidence
Population
Replicable
Unit economics
  • 2.1

    Good enough?

    Does the solution work? Show me the evidence chain — and where it has gaps.

  • 2.2

    Big enough?

    Will solving the problem move a population-level outcome? Or are we describing a slice that doesn't matter at the national level?

  • 2.3

    Simple enough?

    Can a stranger, with the manual, replicate this without you? The founder dependency is the single biggest scale killer.

  • 2.4

    Cheap enough?

    Can the ultimate payer afford to reach everyone? Pilot-scale unit economics do not translate. Scale-scale unit economics must.

  • 2.5

    Doer & Payer at scale

    The two questions Mulago will ask before anything else. Who, specifically, is the entity that implements your model at scale? Who pays them, sustainably, to do it?

Module 03

Impact evidence.

Designing an evidence plan that's defensible to a skeptical funder — not just to a friendly evaluator.

Day 02 · Tuesday · 14:00 – 16:30

Most organizations measure too much, and the wrong things. We design from the outcome backwards: what is the smallest set of metrics that, taken together, force a funder to believe the model is causing the outcome?

  • 3.1

    Levels of evidence

    The ladder from anecdote to RCT. Where on the ladder are you? Where do you actually need to be?

  • 3.2

    Metrics hierarchy

    Outputs, outcomes, impact. Most organizations confuse these — and pay for it during funder due diligence.

  • 3.3

    Designing your evidence plan

    A 12-month, costed plan that closes your most expensive evidence gap. Built in workshop format, with critique.

Module 04

The iterative
organization.

Theory, methods, data flows, and culture. How organizations actually improve at the speed scale requires.

Day 04 · Thursday · 09:00 – 17:00

A model designed for scale is necessary but not sufficient. Scale-ready organizations also improve at scale — they learn from data, kill what isn't working, and double down on what is, on a quarterly rhythm not an annual one.

  • 4.1

    Why iteration matters

    Theory of why this is the difference between organizations that scale and organizations that grow.

  • 4.2

    Iteration methods

    From A/B testing program design to operational quarterly rhythms. Concrete patterns from organizations who do it well.

  • 4.3

    Data flows

    What data flows up, down, and across the organization — and on what cadence — for iteration to be possible.

  • 4.4

    People & culture

    The hardest part. What does it look like inside the org to be one that genuinely runs on iteration, not on heroics?

Module 05

Comms & pitching.

"The pitch is dead. Long live the conversation." Building a narrative that holds up across a 90-second pitch and a 90-minute meeting.

Day 05 · Friday · 09:00 – 17:00

By Day 5, every fellow has a defensible model. This module is about being able to communicate it — first to themselves, then to peers, then to a funder who's heard 200 pitches this year.

  • 5.1

    Clear language

    The discipline of plain language. What jargon you can keep, what you must drop, and why most decks fail on slide three.

  • 5.2

    Narrative structure

    The four-beat structure every memorable funder pitch follows. Designed, not improvised.

  • 5.3

    The 10-minute pitch

    Built live, in workshop. Every fellow drafts theirs on Day 5 and delivers it on Day 6.

The banned words list · examples

empower holistic solution at-risk transform impactful capacity-building ecosystem stakeholders leverage synergy cutting-edge

By Day 6, the pitch has been rehearsed, critiqued, rebuilt, and delivered. The skill the cohort actually walks away with is not the pitch itself — it's the ability to rebuild it for any audience, in any room, for the next ten years.
