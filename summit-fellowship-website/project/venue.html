The venue

Where the
work happens.

Khoj is deliberately remote. The flight in lands at 2,228 m. The drive from the airstrip threads the Shigar river through poplar groves and apricot orchards. By the time you arrive, your phone is no longer the most interesting thing in the room.

The resort is small by design — built for groups exactly this size. Workshop spaces flow onto a stone courtyard. Walk five minutes in any direction and you're in the orchards, on the river bank, or at the foot of a rock face that did not exist on yesterday's horizon.

Days alternate between structured studio sessions, outdoor 1-on-1 clinics, and the kind of unstructured time that only happens when eleven founders find themselves in a valley together with nothing else to do.

The spaces

Five rooms.
One valley.

You'll move between these five spaces over the week. Each was chosen for what it lets the group do, and for what it forbids — Khoj does not have a giant ballroom for a reason.

01

The Studio

Workshop room with U-shaped seating for 12. Whiteboard walls. Where the Mulago core blocks happen, and the one-pagers get written.

02

The Courtyard

Stone courtyard with apricot trees. Where coffee breaks become 30 minutes longer than scheduled, and 1-on-1 clinics spill out when the weather allows.

03

The Dining Hall

One long table. Locally-sourced food. By Day 3, everyone has a permanent seat. By Day 6, no one wants to leave it.

04

The Library Lounge

Fireplace, mismatched armchairs, surprisingly well-stocked shelves. Default home for fireside chats and post-dinner spillover.

05

The Orchard

A short walk from the main building. The default outdoor classroom for 1-on-1 clinics. The cohort's photo gets taken here on Day 6.

06

Off-site: Shigar Fort

15-minute drive. 17th-century Balti fort. Where the Lightning Talks happen on Day 2 — and where the photos every cohort posts come from.

No. 07 · Weather in June

Warm sun, cool nights, clear sky.

June is one of the most favourable months for Shigar. Days are dry and bright with strong mountain sun. Mornings and evenings cool down sharply. Karakoram visibility is typically excellent.

No. 08 · Room allocation

Loft villa, triple sharing.

Fellows are accommodated in Khoj's River-View Loft Villas. Each room sleeps three of the same gender — one bed on the ground floor, two on the loft. Sort the bunk politics among yourselves.

No. 09 · Travel arrangements

We fly you to Skardu. You handle the airport.

All fellows have flight bookings from their respective cities. You're responsible for getting to your departure airport in your home city; our logistics team — coordinated with Ascender Adventures — picks you up at Skardu airport and drives the group to Shigar. Return is mirrored.

No. 11 · Connectivity

Wi-Fi yes, fast no.

Khoj has Wi-Fi, but connectivity in this part of Pakistan isn't always reliable or fast. Cellular coverage from major networks is generally available; 3G/4G works but varies with weather.

  • Resort Wi-FiAvailable
  • Mobile data3G / 4G

No. 12 · Packing list

What to throw in the bag.

Pack for the weather in Section 07. Days are warm, evenings get chilly — at least one warm layer is non-negotiable. Sun protection during the day is the other non-negotiable.

Clothing5

  • One warm layer (fleece / light jacket)
  • Light, breathable daywear
  • Comfortable walking shoes
  • Modest casual wear for village visits
  • Traditional attire for Cultural Night

Sun & weather4

  • Sun hat with brim
  • Sunglasses (UV)
  • SPF 30+ sunscreen
  • Lip balm with SPF

Personal5

  • Refillable water bottle
  • Personal medication (full course)
  • Toiletries
  • Power bank
  • Universal adapter (Pakistan: Type C/D)

Work & bring4

  • Laptop & charger
  • Notebook + pen
  • Headphones
  • A snack or delicacy from your region (Cultural Night)
A note from the team Don't overpack. The valley does not judge wardrobe rotation. The thing you'll wish you'd brought is the warm layer at 9pm on the courtyard — everything else, we can sort.
