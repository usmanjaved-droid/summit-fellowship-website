## 1. Dr. Rubeena Kidwai — Taskeen Health Initiative

**Big Idea:** Mental health helpline for Pakistan

**Sector:** Mental Health  
**Organization:** Taskeen Health Initiative  
**Geography:** Pakistan (national)  
**Structure:** Nonprofit

**The Problem:**
Pakistan has one of the world's lowest psychiatrist-to-population ratios, with fewer than 500 psychiatrists serving over 240 million people. Mental illness carries deep stigma, suicide remains criminalized in practice, and affordable care is concentrated in a few private clinics in major cities. Most Pakistanis with depression, anxiety, or psychosis never reach a trained professional, and rural and low-income populations are almost entirely unserved by formal mental health services.

**Mission:**
Make quality mental health care accessible across Pakistan.

**How It Works:**
Taskeen runs a free national tele-mental-health helpline staffed by trained wellness counselors who provide compassionate listening, screening, and triage Monday through Saturday, 11am to 11pm. Callers needing clinical care are referred to a vetted registry of psychologists and psychiatrists; the Sahara program uses zakat funds to subsidize therapy and psychiatric treatment for those who cannot afford it. Taskeen also runs an AI-powered chatbot, public awareness campaigns like MILKAR with the Pakistan Mental Health Coalition, advocacy work on mental health regulation, and partnerships with institutions such as the Health Services Academy to train more providers.

## 2. Azima Dhanjee — ConnectHear

**Big Idea:** AI-powered sign language for the Deaf

**Sector:** Disability  
**Organization:** ConnectHear  
**Geography:** Punjab, Sindh  
**Structure:** For-profit

**The Problem:**
Pakistan has an estimated 10 million Deaf and hard-of-hearing people, most of whom never receive formal sign language education or interpretation services. Pakistan Sign Language (PSL) is not standardized in schools, hospitals, courts, or public offices, and qualified interpreters are scarce. The Deaf community is largely excluded from healthcare, education, employment, and civic life because almost no institution can communicate with them.

**Mission:**
Make Pakistan accessible to its Deaf community.

**How It Works:**
ConnectHear offers on-demand sign language interpretation in Pakistan Sign Language, both in person and through a video-relay app, so Deaf users can communicate with doctors, banks, employers, and government offices. It sells corporate inclusion services such as accessibility audits, PSL training, and interpretation contracts to 150+ companies, which cross-subsidizes free services for individual Deaf users. The team is building AI tools that translate audio to PSL and PSL video to text. ConnectHear also runs awareness initiatives like the Ishara Theatre Competition. Founded by Azima Dhanjee, a Child of Deaf Adults, with co-founders Arhum Ishtiaq and Areej Al Medinah.

## 3. Habiba Banu — Spiro

**Big Idea:** Preventive TB treatment for children in households

**Sector:** Health  
**Organization:** Spiro  
**Geography:** Pakistan (Punjab focus)  
**Structure:** Nonprofit

**The Problem:**
Pakistan has the fifth highest tuberculosis burden in the world, with over 600,000 new cases a year. Roughly four out of five people who would benefit from TB preventive therapy never receive it, and household contacts of active cases, especially young children, are routinely missed by an overstretched public health system. TB remains the world's deadliest infectious disease and Pakistan accounts for a disproportionate share of preventable deaths.

**Mission:**
Prevent TB deaths in Pakistan's highest-burden communities.

**How It Works:**
Spiro funds and runs community-based contact screening for tuberculosis. When a patient is diagnosed with active TB, Spiro's field teams trace household contacts, screen them, and start eligible contacts, especially children under five, on TB preventive treatment. The work is delivered in Pakistan through local partners and integrated with the national TB program. Spiro is a registered nonprofit incubated through Charity Entrepreneurship and fiscally sponsored by Players Philanthropy Fund. Co-founded in 2023 by Habiba Banu, a former 80,000 Hours staffer and qualified barrister, and Roxanne Heston.

## 4. Saad Hussain — Awaaz-e-Sehat Foundation

**Big Idea:** Voice AI For Pakistani Maternal Care

**Sector:** Health  
**Organization:** Awaaz-e-Sehat Foundation  
**Geography:** Punjab  
**Structure:** Nonprofit

**The Problem:**
Pakistan has one of the highest maternal mortality rates in South Asia, with most deaths occurring among rural and low-literacy women who never get continuous antenatal care. Frontline clinicians and lady health workers are overwhelmed, paper records are lost, and most patient communication tools assume English literacy and typed text. Roman Urdu and spoken Urdu, the languages people actually use, are unsupported by mainstream health software.

**Mission:**
Cut maternal deaths in low-resource Pakistani clinics.

**How It Works:**
Awaaz-e-Sehat is a speech-first AI platform built at LUMS that lets clinicians dictate consultations in Urdu and get structured electronic medical records, decision support, and clinical dashboards in return. The stack is built on fine-tuned Whisper for Urdu speech recognition and an LLM backend with medical dictionaries. A WhatsApp voice chatbot delivers antenatal guidance to pregnant women in Roman Urdu, and the platform supports traditional birth attendants in rural communities. Deployed at Shalamar Hospital in Lahore with rural rollouts underway. Saad Hussain is a co-founder leading ML/NLP, alongside Dr. Maryam Mustafa (LUMS) and Dr. Fozia Qureshi (Shalamar).

## 5. Lala Rukh Fazal-Ur-Rahman — Science Fuse

**Big Idea:** Learning-by-doing science shows for children

**Sector:** Science Education  
**Organization:** Science Fuse  
**Geography:** Sindh, Punjab, KP  
**Structure:** Hybrid

**The Problem:**
Pakistani science education is overwhelmingly rote: students memorize textbooks for exams and rarely conduct an experiment, ask a question, or meet a working scientist. Curricula are theoretical, labs in low-income public and private schools are minimal or nonexistent, and girls in particular drop out of STEM pathways early. The country produces few scientists per capita and most children never see science as something they could do.

**Mission:**
Turn Pakistani children into curious, capable scientists.

**How It Works:**
Science Fuse runs hands-on, inquiry-based science programs for children aged 4 to 14, delivered through workshops, after-school clubs, camps, and large public science festivals. It works with three audiences in parallel: low-fee and government schools, including in places like Karachi's Machar Colony; elite private schools whose fees cross-subsidize free programs; and families directly through Curious Scientists and Digital Science Club. A teaching fellowship trains young Pakistani science communicators to deliver content, and a teacher training arm spreads inquiry-based pedagogy. The organization operates as a social enterprise across Pakistan, founded in 2017 by Lala Rukh Fazal-Ur-Rahman after years at the Science Museum London and Forskerfabrikken in Norway.

## 6. Khushbakht Shah Jillani — Mehfooz AI

**Big Idea:** AI Justice For Pakistani Women

**Sector:** Legal  
**Organization:** Mehfooz AI  
**Geography:** Pakistan (national)  
**Structure:** For-profit

**The Problem:**
Pakistan's family courts are progressive on paper but patriarchal in practice. Women facing domestic violence, divorce, or custody disputes navigate an opaque system in which judges and lawyers vary wildly in their handling of trauma, evidence, and the law. Most women cannot afford competent counsel, do not understand their rights under the Nikahnama or family law, and are routinely failed by courts that miss lethality indicators or apply biased reasoning.

**Mission:**
Make Pakistan's family courts work for women.

**How It Works:**
Mehfooz AI builds two layers of legal technology. For women: free public tools that analyze a Nikahnama, calculate dowry rights, schedule custody, and check eligibility for domestic violence remedies, all in accessible language. For courts: an agentic decision-support system that ingests laws, medical reports, and affidavits, flags missing evidence and lethality indicators, and generates trauma-informed briefs in plain language for judges. The platform is piloting in five district courts and draws on case data from 32 jurisdictions to flag judicial bias. Founded by Khushbakht Shah Jillani, a Karachi family lawyer behind landmark matrimonial property judgments and the nonprofit Adal Aur Sehat.

## 7. Maira Siddiqui — Chiragh Education Technologies

**Big Idea:** Cartoons Teaching Pakistan's Children To Read

**Sector:** Education  
**Organization:** Chiragh Education Technologies  
**Geography:** Sindh, Punjab, KP  
**Structure:** For-profit

**The Problem:**
Pakistan has the second largest out-of-school child population in the world, over 25 million children. Around 58% of Pakistani children cannot read or write Urdu, the national language, and indigenous languages like Sindhi, Punjabi, Pashto, Balochi, and Saraiki have almost no quality early learning content. Most digital education is in English and assumes a literate parent and a stable device, which excludes the majority of underserved Pakistani households.

**Mission:**
Teach every Pakistani child to read in their mother tongue.

**How It Works:**
Chiragh produces gamified, video-based learning content for nursery through grade 5 in Urdu, Sindhi, Punjabi, Pashto, Balochi, Saraiki, and English. Children learn phonics, literacy, and core curriculum through animated characters, songs, and mobile games distributed free on YouTube and through partner foundations that run out-of-school learning programs, including Teach The World Foundation. Schools and NGOs license the full curriculum and games. The model combines free mass-reach content for poor households with paid licensing and corporate sponsorship to underwrite production. Co-founded and led by Maira Siddiqui, an MBA silver medallist and former TCF and Maidan staffer.

## 8. Adnan Qureshi — Teach the World Foundation

**Big Idea:** Tablet-Based Literacy For Out-Of-School Children

**Sector:** Education  
**Organization:** Teach the World Foundation  
**Geography:** Sindh, Punjab, Balochistan  
**Structure:** Nonprofit

**The Problem:**
Over 25 million Pakistani children are out of school and millions more sit in classrooms without learning to read. Government schools in rural Sindh and other provinces face teacher shortages, overcrowding, and weak pedagogy. Traditional school construction is slow and expensive. Most existing edtech requires a trained teacher, stable internet, and English literacy, which doesn't match the reality of underserved Pakistani communities.

**Mission:**
Get every Pakistani child to functional literacy and numeracy.

**How It Works:**
Teach the World Foundation delivers self-paced literacy and numeracy from kindergarten through grade 5 through curated digital learning games on tablets and smartphones. Three delivery models: InSchool, where TTWF tech is layered into existing government and low-fee schools; MicroSchools, one-room digital learning centers in remote communities run by local facilitators; and At-Home, for families with their own devices. Independent evaluations show students achieve roughly 1.5x the learning gains of traditional classrooms. In Pakistan, TTWF partners with the Government of Sindh on 125+ deployments reaching 12,500+ students. Adnan Qureshi, Karachi-based VP and Harvard Ed.M., leads content, tech stack, and special projects.

## 9. Muhammad Waqas — WonderTree

**Big Idea:** AR Games For Children With Disabilities

**Sector:** Special Education  
**Organization:** WonderTree  
**Geography:** Sindh, Punjab, KP  
**Structure:** For-profit

**The Problem:**
Pakistan has an estimated several million children with intellectual, developmental, or physical disabilities, and almost no public infrastructure to serve them. Special education schools are few, costly, and concentrated in major cities. Trained occupational and physical therapists are scarce, and families are left without affordable tools to support their child's cognitive and motor development at home or in school.

**Mission:**
Help special-needs children learn through play.

**How It Works:**
WonderTree builds augmented reality games that turn any room with a phone or laptop camera into a therapy and learning environment for children aged 4 to 14 with mild to moderate disabilities. The games use AI and human pose estimation to track movement and adapt difficulty, training motor skills, cognition, and academic concepts. Schools, therapy centers, and parents subscribe to the platform; therapists and special educators use it as a tool inside sessions, and families use it at home. WonderTree has reached 5,000+ children and is backed by the UNICEF Innovation Fund and Google. Co-founded by Muhammad Usman, Ahmed Hassan, and Muhammad Waqas, who serves as CEO.

## 10. Ali Siddiq — Amal Academy

**Big Idea:** Experiential career fellowship

**Sector:** Livelihoods  
**Organization:** Amal Academy  
**Geography:** Sindh  
**Structure:** Hybrid

**The Problem:**
Pakistan produces hundreds of thousands of graduates a year, but employers consistently say young people lack the communication, problem-solving, and professional skills needed for the workplace. The gap is widest for graduates of low-tier public universities and low-income youth, who have networks and credentials but no practical training. Most end up underemployed, informally employed, or unemployed despite a college degree.

**Mission:**
Launch low-income Pakistani youth into meaningful careers.

**How It Works:**
Amal Academy runs a 3-month career-prep fellowship that teaches communication, critical thinking, problem solving, and professional habits through experiential learning. Fellows complete projects, get feedback from trained facilitators, and build a peer network. The program is delivered in-person in cities like Lahore and online to reach youth across Pakistan, with subsidized fees for low-income participants and partnerships with universities and employers. Over 7,000 young people have completed the fellowship. Amal also runs school programs to embed these skills earlier. Co-founded by Benje Williams, Kunal Chawla, and Ali Siddiq; Ali joined as COO in 2017 and became CEO in 2020 after a career at American Airlines, Amazon, and MathWorks.

## 11. Osama Shahid — Soby Trading Co

**Big Idea:** Locally-manufactured machinery for small farms

**Sector:** Agriculture  
**Organization:** Soby Trading Co  
**Geography:** Balochistan, KP  
**Structure:** For-profit

**The Problem:**
Most Pakistani farmers operate on smallholdings of under five acres, too small to justify a conventional tractor but too labor-intensive to remain profitable as rural wages rise and young workers leave farming. The result is stagnant yields, low margins, and a steady exit from agriculture. Imported machinery is expensive and ill-suited to small fields, while the local equipment market is fragmented and informal.

**Mission:**
Mechanize Pakistan's smallholder farms affordably.

**How It Works:**
Soby Agro Machinery designs, manufactures, and distributes low-cost machinery sized for Pakistani smallholder farms. The flagship product is a three-wheel mini tractor with matched implements (tillers, seeders, reapers, sprayers), plus a wider catalog of dairy machinery, post-harvest equipment, and gardening tools. The company sells direct through showrooms in Sialkot, Lahore, and Karachi and through a dealer network, with after-sales service and farmer training. Soby was a CNFA PATTA supply-side partner and graduated from the LUMS National Incubation Centre. Osama Shahid, founder and CEO, is based in Sialkot, has a decade in Pakistani agri-mechanization, and is an Acumen Fellow (2023).
